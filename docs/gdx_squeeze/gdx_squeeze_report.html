Verdict

Weak evidence at best — not distinguishable from chance with confidence.

  • Selected rule (chosen in-sample out of 350 variants, 119 with ≥20 trades): BB-in-KC, filter event≤10d, exit hold 10d. In-sample: 40 trades, expectancy 0.2%, Sharpe 0.07. Out-of-sample: 13 trades, expectancy 5.5%, Sharpe 1.54, CAGR 24.0% vs buy-and-hold 44.3%.
  • Nothing worked in-sample. The best in-sample Sharpe among the 119 eligible variants is only 0.07 (9% of all variants had positive IS expectancy). Choosing the 'best' of a flat, noisy surface is close to choosing at random; any OOS strength then says more about the OOS period than about the rule.
  • Versus chance: the OOS expectancy beats 98% of 1,000 random-entry runs with the same exit (and 97% of random entries restricted to days when the filter was on); OOS Sharpe beats 98%. In-sample it beat 56% — but in-sample is where it was picked from 350 candidates, so that number is inflated by selection. A 95th-percentile result is the bar a single pre-registered test would need; after a 350-way search the honest bar is far higher.
  • Which filter adds the most: judged by the smaller of its IS and OOS expectancy uplift over the unfiltered BB-in-KC signal, realyld<MA50 adds the most (IS 0.1%, OOS 1.4% per trade). 2 of 4 single filters help in both halves. Filters cut the trade count sharply, so each row rests on fewer trades.
  • Parameter sensitivity: 3% of the 36 percentile×hold cells are positive in-sample and 100% out-of-sample; the IS/OOS cell correlation is -0.28. The in-sample peak is isolated (neighbours average less than half of it) — a classic overfitting signature. A low or negative IS/OOS correlation means the shape of the surface does not persist.
  • Longer history (US GDX): on 2006–2015 data that was never used for selection, the same rule made 32 trades with expectancy -0.4% (beats 48% of random entries). That does not confirm the GDX.L result.
  • Events: on GDX.L, 66% of the big post-squeeze moves (>2 ATR within 10 days) had an FOMC/CPI release in the window — but 66% of all 10-day windows contain one, because there are ~20 such releases a year. P(big | event) = 61% vs P(big | no event) = 72% (Fisher p = 0.56). On US GDX: 73% event-driven vs 65% base rate. "Event-driven" is therefore mostly a base-rate artefact; the event filter is nearly always on and barely filters.

What would make it fail

  • Sample size. The OOS window is ~3.1 years with 13 trades; a single gold-miner rally or crash moves the result more than the signal does.
  • Regime dependence. GDX is a ~2–3× levered bet on gold. The OOS window overlaps a historic gold bull market, so any long-only rule looks good in absolute terms; the random-entry comparison, not CAGR, is the test that matters.
  • Direction-agnostic signal, long-only trade. A squeeze predicts that volatility will expand, not which way. The trade only profits when the expansion is upward — which is mostly a bet on the gold trend, i.e. on the filters, not on the squeeze.
  • Selection bias. 350 variants were tested; the best of many noisy backtests always looks good. The full grid is shown so you can see the spread.
  • Execution on GDX.L. The LSE line is thinly traded (early history has no-trade days and vendor bars with Open outside High/Low). Real spreads at the open can exceed the 0.15% assumed; the USD line is also quoted while the US market is shut, so the London open often just reprices the prior US close.
  • Stops on daily bars. The ATR target/stop assumes fills at the level unless the open gaps through it, and assumes the stop is hit first when a bar touches both.

7. Method, data and lookahead guards

  • Data. yfinance daily, auto-adjusted OHLC (GDX.L is accumulating so adjustment is a no-op; US GDX is dividend-adjusted). Gold = GC=F front-month future (roll gaps are present but only its 200-day MA is used). DXY = DX-Y.NYB. 10y TIPS real yield = FRED DFII10. ^TNX downloaded and aligned for reference but not used in a rule. Raw files in data/gdx_squeeze/raw/; pass --refresh to re-download. Cleaning:
    • GDX.L: 2916 raw bars; dropped 1 incomplete/non-positive, 40 zero-volume no-range bars; repaired 3 bars with Open outside High/Low.
    • GDX (US): 5129 raw bars; dropped 0 incomplete/non-positive, 0 zero-volume no-range bars; repaired 0 bars with Open outside High/Low.
  • Event calendar. FOMC from federalreserve.gov (historical pages for 2006–2020, current calendar after): 175 scheduled decisions, 5 unscheduled meetings, and 17 conference calls / notation votes (kept in events.csv but not treated as events; note this drops the emergency cuts decided on calls in Jan and Oct 2008, which predate GDX.L). CPI: 248 release dates. bls.gov returns HTTP 403 to scripted clients, so the dates are rebuilt from ALFRED's archive of BLS's CPIAUCSL: a release date is the first vintage date on which a new month's CPI appears. This reproduces the 2013 and 2025 shutdown delays. Saved as data/gdx_squeeze/events.csv. Gaps:
    • FOMC 2020: 7 scheduled meetings found (8 is normal) - the March 17-18 meeting was cancelled (replaced by the unscheduled March 15 cut).
    • CPI 2025: 11 releases found (expected ~12) - the October 2025 CPI was never published because of the federal shutdown; September's CPI was delayed to Oct 24.
  • Signals evaluated at close t, entry at open t+1. BB(20, 2σ) width percentile is a trailing 252-day rank including today; the exit median is a trailing 252-day median. Keltner = 20-day SMA ± 1.5 × 20-day ATR (same mid as Bollinger, so "BB inside KC" ⇔ 2σ < 1.5 ATR). z-score = 60-day z of log close.
  • Macro alignment. Each series' moving average is computed on its own calendar, forward-filled onto GDX.L sessions (never backfilled), then lagged one session, because US closes print after the London close and DFII10 for day t is published on t+1.
  • Event filter uses only scheduled FOMC meetings and CPI release dates, which are published in advance — no knowledge of unscheduled actions.
  • Trades. Long only, one position at a time, 0.15% round-trip cost (half on entry, half on exit day). Fixed holds exit at the close of the Nth session (entry day = 1). ATR exit: +2 ATR target / −1 ATR stop from the entry price, ATR as of the signal close; gaps through a level fill at the open; if a bar touches both, the stop is assumed first; 40-session time stop. BBW exit: sell at the next open after a close with width above its trailing median.
  • Split. Usable history (after the 272-session warm-up) split 70/30 by sessions. Entries are confined to each window; a trade opened in-sample may finish a few days into OOS. Selection = highest in-sample Sharpe among variants with ≥20 in-sample trades.
  • Random baseline. Each run enters on random sessions (Bernoulli, probability set so the expected trade count matches the strategy) and uses the same exit rule, window and costs. A second set restricts random entries to days when the selected filter was on, isolating what the squeeze adds on top of the filter.
  • Not modelled: FX (the USD line is used, so no GBP conversion), borrow/financing, slippage beyond the flat cost, capacity.
