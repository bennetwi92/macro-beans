# Five-Year ISA Growth Proposal — £20,000 Transfer

This proposal sets out three diversified, fund-based portfolios for a ~£20,000 Stocks & Shares ISA with a five-year horizon and a single full withdrawal at the end. Each option is built by the in-house portfolio optimiser, maximises return for its risk level, deliberately under-weights today's crowded mega-cap-technology/AI trade, and tilts toward out-of-favour themes with structural growth potential. The recommended option is **Balanced Growth**.

## 3. How the portfolios are built (investment process)

We use the same disciplined process for all three options:

1. **Forward-looking return assumptions, not past performance.** Expected returns are built from transparent "building blocks" — a cash rate, an equity-risk premium, and factor premia (value, size, quality, emerging-market, theme) — net of each fund's ongoing charge. We never extrapolate a fund's recent returns. These are a *defensible prior*, not a forecast, and the optimiser is stress-tested against them being wrong.
2. **Risk estimated from long history.** We estimate how the holdings move together using ~17 years of monthly returns (from 2009), with young funds mapped onto longer-history proxies, and a shrinkage technique that stops the estimate from over-fitting.
3. **Diversification is rewarded explicitly.** The optimiser maximises *geometric* (compounded) growth, which penalises volatility — so a holding earns its place by improving the portfolio, not by having a high headline return in isolation. This is why gold and a trend fund earn slots despite modest standalone returns: they cushion the falls.
4. **Robust weights, not a fragile "optimal" point.** A naïve optimiser piles into a few holdings and is wildly sensitive to small input changes. We instead use **resampling**: we re-run the optimisation hundreds of times on noise-perturbed inputs and average the result. The output is diversified and stable.
5. **Validated by simulation.** We run 20,000 Monte-Carlo paths to estimate the five-year outcome range and the worst drawdowns, and we report the tail honestly.

> **Why this fits your "no hot themes" brief.** The equity core is built from **value**, **small-cap value**, **quality**, **equal-weight S&P 500** and **emerging markets**. Equal-weighting the S&P 500 deliberately removes the mega-cap-technology concentration that dominates a standard index; value and emerging markets are the parts of the global market trading at the *cheapest* valuations today. There is **no Nasdaq-100, no semiconductor, and no "AI" fund** anywhere in the universe. The portfolios are, by construction, a tilt *away* from the crowded trade.

## 9. What you would be invested in — the "interesting themes" story

A fair question is *"am I just buying a global tracker with extra steps?"* No — these portfolios look deliberately different from a standard index fund, in the directions you asked for:

- **Cheap over expensive.** The core is value and small-cap value, globally. Today that means under-owning the handful of very large, very expensive US technology companies that dominate a normal index.
- **Equal-weight US.** Where we do hold the S&P 500, we hold the **equal-weighted** version, so a £1 in Apple counts the same as a £1 in a mid-sized industrial. This single choice removes most of the "AI mega-cap" concentration risk.
- **Emerging markets.** ~8–15% in emerging markets — among the cheapest equity on valuation, and a genuine diversifier from US/developed markets.
- **Real assets.** Gold and listed infrastructure are real-asset hedges that tend to do relatively well when inflation surprises or markets wobble — neither is a "growth-story" bet.
- **Two structural, under-owned themes.** European **defence/rearmament** and **nuclear/uranium** — both driven by multi-year structural shifts (security, energy independence, decarbonisation), both currently far from the spotlight that sits on AI. They are the "interesting, undervalued, future-growth" sleeve you asked for, sized as satellites.

## 10. Risks and important considerations

Please read this section as carefully as the return tables.

- **Capital is at risk.** You could get back less than £20,000. In a poor five years the modelled value falls to roughly £18,000 at the 5th percentile, and short-term paper losses of 25–30%+ are entirely possible — especially for Options 1 and 2.
- **The projections are modelled, not promised.** Outcomes come from simulation using forward-looking assumptions. If those assumptions are wrong, the outcomes will differ. Past performance is not a guide to the future.
- **Tail risk is understated.** Our simulations assume "normally" distributed returns, which under-state genuine crashes (2008, 2020, a future AI-bubble unwind). Real falls can be deeper and faster than the tables suggest. The gold, trend and infrastructure sleeves in Options 2–3 are the deliberate hedge against exactly this; Option 1 has the least protection.
- **Sequence risk at a fixed exit.** Because you will sell everything at year five, a crash in year four or five is far more damaging than the same crash in year one — you may not have time to recover. This is the single most important reason we recommend a diversified option and a **glidepath** (see §11), not the all-equity option.
- **Theme concentration.** Defence and uranium are sector bets and can fall sharply and persistently. They are capped for this reason; do not increase them beyond the model weights without understanding the added risk.
- **Currency.** Most underlying assets are non-GBP; the value in pounds moves with exchange rates. The trend fund is GBP-hedged; gold and overseas equities are not.
- **Fund-specific points to verify before funding.** A few holdings are distributing (not accumulating) share classes — harmless inside an ISA, but you'll receive small cash dividends to reinvest. Confirm each ticker is **searchable and tradable inside your Trading 212 ISA** before you build the Pie (in particular AVCG.L, NAVY.L, URNP.L and the JMFP managed-futures fund); the optimiser includes same-role substitutes if any one is unavailable.

## 11. Implementation and the five-year glidepath

**Transferring the money (do this right).**
- Use an **ISA transfer**, not a withdraw-and-redeposit. Ask Trading 212 to pull the funds from Nutmeg via the official ISA transfer process. This keeps the money inside the ISA wrapper and means it **does not use up your current-year £20,000 ISA allowance** — that allowance stays free for new savings.
- Previous-year ISA money can be transferred in full without affecting this year's allowance. Expect the transfer to take a couple of weeks.

**Building the portfolio.**
- Create one **Trading 212 Pie** and add a slice per holding at the percentages in your chosen option. Turn on auto-invest/auto-rebalance if you wish.
- Invest the lump sum in one go, or — if markets feel stretched — phase it in over 2–4 months to reduce the risk of buying at a short-term peak. Over a five-year horizon, investing promptly is usually the higher-expected-value choice; phasing is a behavioural comfort, not a return enhancer.

**Maintenance.**
- **Rebalance** back to target roughly once or twice a year (the Pie can do this automatically). Inside an ISA there is no capital-gains tax on rebalancing, so it is essentially free to keep the weights on track.
- **Review annually**: re-confirm the assumptions still make sense and that the themes still have a structural case. The optimiser can be re-run to refresh the numbers.

**The glidepath (important for a fixed exit).**
Because you will withdraw everything at year five, plan to **de-risk progressively in the final 12–24 months** — shifting from the Balanced option toward the All-Weather mix, and ultimately into short bonds / cash-like holdings as the date approaches. This locks in gains and removes the risk that a late crash forces you to sell at the bottom. A simple schedule:

| Time to withdrawal | Suggested stance |
|---|---|
| 5–2 years | Balanced Growth (Option 2) |
| ~2 years | Begin shifting toward All-Weather (Option 3) |
| ~12 months | Majority in defensive equity / gold / short bonds |
| ~3–6 months | Largely cash-like ballast (IGLS.L / ERNS.L); protect the number |

### Appendix A — Methodology and assumptions

*Generated by the in-house `portfolio_optimiser` (module `report.build_isa_5y`). Inputs are editable in `portfolio_optimiser/config/`; re-run with `python -m portfolio_optimiser.report.build_isa_5y` to refresh.*

- **Return history:** month-end GBP total returns, Feb 2009 – Jun 2026, with young funds spliced onto longer-history proxies.
- **Expected returns:** forward-looking building-block capital-market assumptions, net of each fund's TER; converted from arithmetic to geometric using the modelled variance.
- **Risk:** Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage covariance.
- **Weights:** Michaud-resampled (250 draws) for estimation robustness; sub-3% "dust" removed; per-holding and per-sleeve caps applied.
- **Validation:** 20,000-path Monte-Carlo for the 1-year tail (95% CVaR) and the 5-year drawdown and terminal-wealth distributions. The "passive tracker" benchmark is a transparent global-equity proxy (65% global value + 20% equal-weight S&P 500 + 15% emerging markets) priced at the plain developed-equity assumption of 7.0%.
- **Key constraints by option:** Growth — maximise geometric growth, themes ≤20%, no bonds. Balanced — geometric growth subject to a 1-year 95% CVaR ≤ 15%, no forced bond floor. All-Weather — as Balanced but CVaR ≤ 9% with higher diversifier floors.
