CHEAP OR DEAR

Which markets look cheap right now — and which look stretched? This ranks every instrument by how its price sits versus its own recent history, then shows what buying it cheap did next.

HOW TO READ THIS

Cheap here means cheap versus the market's own past — not cheap against earnings or book value. A market well below its 1-year high and below its 200-day average is in its own bargain bin; one near the top of its 5-year range is dear. The last column is the only piece of history: it shows the typical (middle) move over the following month across every past day this market closed below its 200-day average. A market that drifts ever higher will look "never cheap" — that is the screen working, not a fault.

OFF 1-YR HIGH and VS 200-DAY AVG are how far the last price sits below (or above) those levels. 5-YR RANGE is where the price sits between its 5-year low (0%) and high (100%). NEXT MONTH WHEN CHEAP is history, not a forecast — and a market that is rarely below its average (greyed out) has too few past examples to lean on.
